## Part 2 — Length control: main report versus statistical annex

The brief asks the report to be protected from becoming unnecessarily long. The principle applied: **the main report carries headline indicators, trends, comparisons, regulatory interpretation and interventions with outcomes. Detailed statistics go to the annex.**

### 2.2 Keep in the main report

These earn their place because each carries a regulatory message:

- **Every "At a glance" KPI card set** (one per sector) — the fastest route to "what changed and by how much".
- **The cross-sector overview table** (3.1) — the report's only genuinely comparative view.
- **The Regulatory Insight, Key Achievement and Emerging Risk callouts** — these are where the report does its interpretive work. There are currently seven and they should be kept, but **each needs its `[DATA REQUIRED: RURA action]` field filled**, or it states a problem without stating the regulatory response.
- **All multi-year trend charts** — mobile and internet penetration, water production and subscriptions, LPG imports, bandwidth, pay TV.
- **Year-on-year change charts** — Figure 3.14 (electricity indicators), Figure 3.21 (fleet by category), the licensing comparisons.
- **The Gross Contract Model case study and headway chart** (4.6, Figure 4.1) — the report's strongest evidence of regulatory effect.
- **The water expansion inspection results by district** (population served, schools and health facilities connected) — the only inspection programme in the draft reported with outcomes attached.
- **The nine-risk register** (7.1) once rated.
- **The chapter 2 scorecards** once populated.
- **The four price decisions** (4.3) once supplied.

### 2.3 Already correctly placed in the annex — leave there

The fourteen detailed ICT licensing tables (9.1) are exactly what an annex is for: complete licence-by-licence records for TV stations, FM radio, pay TV, print media, multimedia, online newspapers, courier, earth stations, private mobile radio and FM broadcasting. No change.

### 2.4 One structural addition recommended

**Publish the statistical annex as an Excel workbook alongside the PDF.** The baseline already proposes this. It resolves the length problem permanently: detailed series can be comprehensive in the workbook while the printed annex carries only headline five-year series. It also makes the report's data reusable, which directly serves the objective of a more data-driven institution.

### 2.5 Indicative effect on length

Moving the content in §2.1 removes roughly **fifteen detailed tables and six photographs** from the main report while losing no headline figure and no regulatory interpretation. That creates the room needed for the material that is currently missing — the price decisions, the compliance outcomes, the financial performance section, the electricity market section and the completed callouts — **without the report growing overall.**

That is the trade worth making: the report should get *more analytical* without getting *longer*.
