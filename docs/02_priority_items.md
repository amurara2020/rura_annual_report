# The 15 Highest-Priority Items Required to Complete the FY2025/26 Annual Report

Ranked by *publication risk × effort*. Items 1–4 are corrections or extractions and need no new data collection. Items 5–15 require departmental input and should be requested this week.

**Priority convention used throughout the matrix**
- **Priority 1** — Essential for the FY2025/26 report. The report should not publish without it.
- **Priority 2** — Include if the data can be obtained within the reporting timeline.
- **Priority 3** — Future reporting improvement, FY2026/27 onward.

---

### 1. Resolve the six material numerical contradictions
**Why first:** they are published, checkable, and self-contradictory. Nothing else should be finalised on top of wrong numbers.

| Item | Resolve |
| :-- | :-- |
| Transport complaints 1,770/90% vs 6,716/95% | Which figure, and what "handled" means |
| Nuclear inspections 46 vs 99 medical | 46 appears to be Eastern Province only |
| Nuclear compliance rate 23% vs "high compliance" narrative | Define the rate, then reconcile |
| Nuclear revenue 49.7% vs 33.5% (26.75/79.9) | Recompute or explain the basis |
| Internet 11,193,584 labelled "mobile" | It is mobile + fixed |
| Towers 11 vs 13 | The annex lists 11 permits |

**Owner:** Sector departments (Transport, Nuclear, ICT) · **Effort:** low · **Priority 1**

### 2. Standardise reference periods to June 2025 → June 2026
Mobile money, fixed broadband and market-share series mix March 2026 and June 2026. Mixed periods make every year-on-year change in the ICT chapter non-comparable, and that chapter carries the report's strongest data.

**Owner:** Sector Dept (ICT) + Data Analytics Team · **Effort:** low–medium · **Priority 1**

### 3. Build the five-year statistical annex (9.2)
Energy series currently run two years; transport and nuclear have no annex tables at all. **Without this, no 3–5 year trend chart is possible in three of five sectors** — which is the core of the DG's requirement.

Specifically needed: energy (capacity, generation, peak demand, customers, losses, outages, outage hours, LPG storage) FY2021/22–FY2025/26; transport (licences and authorisations by category, fleet by category, offences, Kigali ridership); nuclear (applications, licences, inspections, revenue); plus international bandwidth for Jun-21 and Jun-22.

**Owner:** Data Analytics Team (assemble) + sector departments (supply history) · **Priority 1**

### 4. Write the indicator dictionary (annex 9.6)
Definition, formula, unit, reference period, disaggregation, source system and data owner for every headline indicator. This is the structural fix for the undefined terms already causing contradictions: "compliance rate 23%", "sector growth 110%", "performance rate 49.7%", "handled" vs "resolved".

**Owner:** Data Analytics Team, with departmental sign-off · **Priority 1**

### 5. The four price decisions of the year (4.3)
Electricity tariff by customer class; combined urban water and sanitation tariff by consumption block; symmetric termination rates; intercity fares. For each: previous level, new level, % change, effective date, expected consumer effect.

All four were taken during the year and are referenced in the draft. **None of the numbers appear anywhere in the report.** A regulator's annual report that omits its own price decisions has a material gap.

**Owner:** Board Secretariat + sector departments (Energy, Water, ICT, Transport) · **Priority 1**

### 6. ICT quality of service results by operator (3.2)
Call drop rate, call setup success rate and data throughput per operator against each licensed threshold, for FY2024/25 and FY2025/26. The draft says only "moderate overall improvement".

This is the largest analytical gap in the ICT chapter and the reason the cross-sector quality row is empty.

**Owner:** Sector Dept (ICT) · **Priority 1**

### 7. The Energy compliance section (3.3) — currently does not exist
Inspections of petrol stations, LPG facilities and electrical installations; fuel quality test results (samples taken, passed, failed, by product); directives, penalties, revocations; compliance rate.

Energy is the only sector with no compliance content whatsoever. Given the sector's safety profile — LPG, petroleum storage, electrical installations — this is the most serious sectoral omission in the report.

**Owner:** Sector Dept (Energy), with RSB for fuel quality results · **Priority 1**

### 8. Release the M&E Framework to the Data Analytics Team
One file unblocks: chapter 2 in full (all 9 goals and all 17 initiatives currently read "Not assessed"), annex 9.4, headline tile 15, and the target markers on every sector KPI card.

The 90%/75% status thresholds proposed in the draft legend also need formal confirmation by Planning.

**Owner:** Corporate — Strategy/Planning · **Priority 1**

### 9. RURA financial performance (6.4) and audited statements (9.3)
Revenue by source, expenditure by category, approved budget vs actual, budget execution rate, UAF income and disbursement, external audit opinion for the last five years.

The section is entirely empty, it blocks headline tile 16, and strategic goal 9 (financial self-sufficiency) cannot be rated without it. Confirm the audit sign-off date early — it may determine the report's publication date.

**Owner:** Corporate — Finance · **Priority 1**

### 10. Complaints data for ICT, Energy, Water and Nuclear (5.1)
Register-level per sector: receipt date, category, closure date, outcome, redress amount — plus the customer/subscriber base per sector so complaints can be normalised per 10,000 customers. Without normalisation, sectors of very different size cannot be compared.

Currently one sector of five is reported, and its two figures contradict each other.

**Owner:** Sector departments (all five) + Data Analytics Team · **Priority 1**

### 11. Secure the transport AFC / e-ticketing data feed
Tap-in records with timestamp, route, stop, operator, vehicle, fare product and amount, for FY2024/25 and FY2025/26, joined to the existing GTFS route dataset.

**Longest lead time of anything on this list and the highest analytical payoff.** It converts the transport chapter from licensing statistics into demand analytics: route-level demand vs capacity, temporal demand profile, operator benchmarking, fleet utilisation, ticketing compliance.

**Owner:** Data Analytics Team (analysis); AFC operator (data); Sector Dept (Transport) to authorise · **Priority 1 for route demand and operator benchmarking; Priority 2 for the temporal heatmap and load factors**

### 12. Water non-revenue water and drinking-water quality compliance (3.4)
NRW % by operator and service area; water quality samples tested and compliant by parameter and operator; average hours of supply.

The draft already establishes that production per subscription is falling (226 → 196 m³/year). Whether that is a production constraint or a loss problem cannot be answered without NRW — and the report currently asserts the trend without being able to explain it.

**Owner:** Sector Dept (Water) + WASAC/licensed operators · **Priority 1**

### 13. Rate and assign the nine risks (7.1), and add institutional risks (7.3)
The evidence column is already complete and quantified. Only likelihood, impact, owner and response are missing — a low-cost, high-value completion. Section 7.3 (institutional risks: financial, staffing, cyber, legal) and lessons learned are entirely empty.

**Owner:** Corporate — Internal Audit/Risk + sector departments · **Priority 1**

### 14. Targets, deadlines and owners for the eleven FY2026/27 priorities (8.1)
Eleven priorities are named with 33 empty target/deadline/owner cells. Without them the chapter cannot be used next year to report against commitments — which is exactly the gap section 2.4 is trying to close for FY2024/25. **Breaking that cycle starts here.**

Also needed: FY2026/27 priorities from ICT and Water, which report none.

**Owner:** Sector departments (all five) + Corporate — Strategy/Planning · **Priority 1**

### 15. The Foreword, and photograph rights
The Foreword is missing entirely (≈600–700 words, portrait, signature, pull quote) and must not repeat the Executive Summary.

Separately: sector banner images appear to come from media websites with no stated licence. Confirm rights or replace with RURA-owned photography before publication.

**Owner:** Corporate — Board Secretariat (Foreword); Corporate — Communication (photography) · **Priority 1**
