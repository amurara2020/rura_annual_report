# Review: Rwanda Telecommunications Market Brief

**For:** Director General
**From:** Data Analytics Team
**Date:** 15 November 2026
**Subject:** Accuracy of data used in the market brief submitted for review

**Sources used for this review:** RURA Annual Report FY 2025/26; RURA ICT Sector Statistics Reports for Q1, Q2, Q3
and Q4 2025 and Q1 and Q2 2026; Rwanda Energy Group (REG); GSMA, as cited by the brief itself.

The Q2 2026 report covers 1 April to 30 June 2026 — the Annual Report's own reference date — so the two can be
compared directly. See §6.

Twenty of the 22 claims are settled from the RURA publications. The two network coverage claims are the exception:
coverage appears in **none** of the six quarterly reports from Q1 2025 to Q2 2026, nor in the Annual Report. Those
figures come from the ICT department, understood to be drawn from the **Q3 2026 report currently in preparation and
not yet published**. This is noted at §1.1, and means they should not be cited externally yet.

---

## Bottom line

The brief is broadly sound in direction but contains **two factual errors**, several unsourced figures, and rests
substantially on figures RURA does not produce.

Of 22 checkable claims: **6 are confirmed** by RURA publications, **2 are wrong and should be corrected**,
**9 are misleading, unsourced or out of date**, and **5 are not RURA indicators** and cannot be validated by the
Authority.

The most significant error is in the brief's own headline coverage table, where the 3G and 4G figures have been
transposed. The most significant omission is Mobile Number Portability, which the brief does not mention at all.

**Recommendation: the Authority should not endorse the brief in its present form.** It should issue the three
corrections below, offer the author RURA's published indicator set, and refer the remaining claims to their own
source holders.

## 1. Figures requiring correction or sourcing

### 1.1 Network coverage — the 3G and 4G figures are transposed

| Technology | Brief states | Correct figure (June 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| 3G population coverage | approximately 99% | **96.53%** |
| 4G population coverage | approximately 96% | **98.69%** |

*Source: ICT department, position as at June 2026, averaged across the reporting operators (MTN, Airtel, KTRN),
understood to come from the Q3 2026 statistics report currently in preparation. Not published — see the note
below.*

Each figure in the brief is within approximately 2.5 percentage points of the **other** technology's true value,
indicating the two have been swapped rather than independently misstated.

This is not a presentational matter. As written, the brief informs readers that 3G reaches a greater share of the
population than 4G. The reverse is the case, and 4G expansion is the principal ICT development of the reporting
year: the 4G share of mobile internet subscriptions rose from 50.9% to 56.8%, on 6.31 million 4G subscriptions, an
increase of 22.8% *(Source: Annual Report FY 2025/26)*.

**Two points before these figures are quoted publicly.**

*First, they are not yet published anywhere.* The quarterly statistics series carried a coverage table until 2017
and no longer does: coverage is absent from all six quarterly reports from Q1 2025 to Q2 2026, and the Annual Report
has never contained one. These figures are understood to come from the Q3 2026 report still in preparation, so they
are draft figures. They should not be cited externally until that report is published, and their reference date
should be confirmed — the figures are given as the June 2026 position but originate in work on a later quarter.

*Second, an average across operators is not national coverage.* National coverage — the share of population reached
by at least one operator — is the union of the operators' footprints and is a different and higher measure. One of
the three operators is also a 4G wholesaler, which affects what the 3G row represents. The Authority should settle
which measure it publishes before a single national coverage figure enters circulation.

### 1.2 Household electricity access — plausible, but unsourced and undated

| | Figure | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| Brief states | approximately 85%–88% | presented as 2026, no source given |
| Latest figure held | **84.6%** | **July 2025** *(REG)* |

**The brief's range may well be correct.** The most recent figure available to us is 84.6% at July 2025, and
electricity access has continued to rise since: RURA's own grid connections grew **13.5%** over FY 2025/26, from
1,768,830 to 2,008,342. A 2026 position of 85–88% is therefore consistent with the direction of travel, and may
even be conservative at the lower bound.

The fault is one of sourcing rather than accuracy. The brief gives neither a source nor a reference date for a
figure it presents as current, and the Authority holds no 2026 figure against which to check it.

**Action:** the current access figure should be confirmed with **REG** and the brief required to cite it with its
date, rather than treated as an error.

The brief's further claim that grid connections represent approximately 60% of households and off-grid access
approximately 25% is unsourced and cannot be confirmed. Those two components sum to 85%, which is the floor of the
brief's own range and leaves its 88% upper bound unsupported by its own arithmetic.

### 1.3 Broadband expansion — wrong in direction for fixed broadband

The brief refers to "continued broadband expansion". Mobile broadband did expand. Fixed broadband declined.

| Indicator | December 2025 | June 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed broadband subscriptions per 100 inhabitants | 0.611 | 0.584 |

*Sources: ICT Sector Statistics Report Q4 2025; Annual Report FY 2025/26.*

The decline appears in both publications independently. Total fixed broadband subscriptions stood at 84,152 at June
2026, following the removal of inactive users.

## 3. Mobile internet adoption — the brief's central claim is not a RURA measure, and does not reconcile with RURA data

The brief states, citing GSMA, that unique mobile internet users represented approximately 21% of the population in
2025, and builds its central argument on that figure — that "digital adoption remains below infrastructure
readiness levels".

### 3.1 RURA does not measure internet users, and has deliberately not done so

RURA counts **connection points**, not people. The published figures are active subscriptions: one individual
holding a phone SIM, a router SIM and a tablet SIM is counted three times. There is no de-duplication, by design.

This is not an oversight. The Authority's own indicator framework lists *"Unique internet users (estimated)"* with
the note *"after de-duplication or modelling"* and **assigns it no collection source**. The two inputs that
de-duplication would require — *"Multi-SIM users"* and the *"dual-SIM vs single-SIM ratio, for SIM de-duplication
modelling"* — likewise carry no collection source, while every metric RURA does collect is mapped to a returns
sheet.

**The Authority therefore cannot produce, confirm or refute any unique-user figure, including 21%.** It is outside
what RURA measures.

### 3.2 The 21% figure does not reconcile with RURA's published data

The figure can nonetheless be tested against RURA's own numbers, and it does not hold:

| | |
|---|---|
| 21% of the population (≈14.42 million) | ≈ **3.03 million** unique users |
| Mobile internet subscriptions, June 2026 | **11,193,584** |
| **Implied subscriptions per user** | **3.70** |
| 4G subscriptions alone, June 2026 | 6,310,000 → **2.08 per implied user** |

*Source: Annual Report FY 2025/26.*

For 21% to be correct, every mobile internet user in Rwanda would hold **3.7 active mobile internet subscriptions on
average**, and more than two 4G subscriptions each. Multi-SIM use is common, but not at that level: at a plausible
1.5 subscriptions per user, a 21% penetration rate would imply roughly 4.5 million subscriptions, where RURA records
11.2 million — a difference of 6.7 million.

The gap cannot be explained by multi-SIM holding alone. It would require some combination of machine-to-machine SIMs
sitting inside RURA's subscription count, a materially narrower GSMA definition than the brief conveys, a different
reference year, or a misreading of the GSMA source. **The brief gives no GSMA edition, report title or publication
year, so none of these can be checked.**

### 3.3 What this means for the brief

The fault is more serious than a missing label. The brief **substitutes an unverifiable third-party model estimate
for a measured regulatory statistic**, and then rests its central conclusion on the estimate. That conclusion may
well be right — RURA's own reporting notes that fixed broadband is marginal and declining — but it is not evidenced
by anything the Authority holds, and RURA cannot endorse it.

**Required of the author:**

1. Cite the GSMA source in full — report title, edition and year — so the figure and its definition can be checked.
2. State plainly that it is a modelled estimate of unique individuals, not a measured count.
3. Do not present it as the adoption position alongside RURA figures as though the two were comparable. RURA
   measures subscriptions per 100 inhabitants; GSMA models individuals. They are different quantities and neither
   converts into the other.
4. Where the brief states RURA's position, use RURA's measure and unit: **11,193,584 mobile internet subscriptions
   at June 2026, equivalent to 77.6 per 100 inhabitants** — not "77.6% of the population", which the figure does
   not mean.

## 6. Assurance on RURA's own figures

The Annual Report's ICT series was cross-checked against the four published 2025 quarterly reports. The two sets of
figures reconcile throughout, allowing for the different reference periods — the quarterly series reports calendar
quarters, the Annual Report reports June to June.

| Indicator | Q4 2025 (31 Dec 2025) | Annual Report (30 Jun 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile-cellular subscriptions | 13,685,892 | 14,501,766 |
| Mobile subscriptions per 100 inhabitants | 97.03 | 100.6 |
| MTN / Airtel market share | 66.3% / 33.7% | 65.8% / 34.2% |
| Internet subscriptions | 10,487,460 | 11,193,584 |
| 4G share of mobile internet | 56.0% | 56.8% |
| Used international bandwidth | 344,907 Mbps | 345,864 Mbps |
| Fixed broadband per 100 inhabitants | 0.611 | 0.584 |

No discrepancy was found between the two publications.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Do not endorse the brief in its present form.** Nine of its 22 checkable claims are not RURA indicators, and
   endorsement would adopt figures from GSMA, REG, NISR and unattributed sources as regulatory data.
2. **Issue the corrections** in §1: the transposed coverage figures and the direction of fixed broadband. The
   electricity access range is plausible and should be sourced and dated rather than corrected — we will confirm
   the current figure with REG.
3. **Require the adoption claim to be sourced and re-stated** (§3). The GSMA figure must carry its report title,
   edition and year; it must be described as a modelled estimate of individuals; and it must not be presented as
   comparable with RURA's subscription figures. RURA does not measure internet users and cannot endorse a
   unique-user figure — and 21% does not reconcile with RURA's own data in any case.
4. **Offer the author RURA's published indicator set**, with prior-year comparators and stated reference dates, so
   that a revised version rests on RURA figures rather than unattributed ones.
5. **Require the regulatory section to be rewritten** to reflect Mobile Number Portability and the two regulations
   made during the year.
6. **Refer the remaining claims** to NISR and MINECOFIN (population, economic growth) and REG (grid and off-grid
   access).
7. **Settle the coverage methodology** — whether the Authority publishes an average across operators or
   any-operator national coverage — before any national coverage figure is released externally.

## 8. One matter for the Authority's own consideration

RURA collects network coverage from every operator each quarter, for 2G, 3G, 4G and 5G, on both a geographic and a
population basis. None of it is published: the quarterly statistics series carried a coverage table until 2017 and
no longer does, and the Annual Report has never contained one.

This is why an external author writing about Rwanda's network coverage relied on GSMA and unattributed sources
rather than on RURA's own figures, and why confirming a claim about coverage in the Authority's own sector required
reference to internal returns.

**Proposal:** restore a coverage table — geographic and population, by technology generation, including 5G — to the
quarterly publication, and carry a summary in the Annual Report. The data is already collected and validated each
quarter, so the cost is editorial rather than analytical.
