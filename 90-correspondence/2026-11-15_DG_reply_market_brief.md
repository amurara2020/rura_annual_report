**Subject:** Review of the Rwanda Telecommunications Market Brief — findings and recommendation

Dear Director General,

We have reviewed the market brief against the FY 2025/26 Annual Report, our published ICT Sector Statistics Reports
for all four quarters of 2025, and the coverage, adoption and electricity-access figures supplied by the SDS in
charge of ICT. The full check is attached; the position in short is below.

**The brief is not fabricated, but much of it is not ours to confirm.** Of its 22 checkable claims, six are
confirmed by our publications, seven are indicators RURA does not publish at all, two concern network coverage
which we collect but no longer publish, three are materially misleading as written, and four are out of date. The
figures most likely to be quoted — network coverage, electricity access, population and economic growth — are the
ones we cannot stand behind, because they belong to GSMA, the World Bank, NISR and EDCL rather than to the
Authority. If RURA is recorded as having reviewed and accepted the brief, we adopt those numbers as regulatory
data.

**On network coverage, the brief has its two figures the wrong way round.** It reports 3G population coverage at
about 99% and 4G at about 96%. The operator returns for June 2026 give **3G at 96.53% and 4G at 98.69%**. Each of
the brief's numbers is within about two and a half points of the *other* technology's true value, which is the
signature of a transposition rather than of independent error. The consequence is not cosmetic: as written, the
brief tells the reader that 3G reaches more of the population than 4G, when the reverse is true — and 4G migration
is the central ICT finding of the year in the Annual Report, where the 4G share of mobile internet rose from 50.9%
to 56.8%.

I should flag one thing before these coverage figures travel further. They are an unweighted average across three
operators' self-reported footprints. National coverage — the share of the population reached by at least one
operator — is the union of those footprints and would be higher than any average, and one of the three operators is
a 4G wholesaler, which affects what the 3G row means. The figures are sound for internal use; I would want the
methodology settled before the Authority puts a single national coverage number into public circulation, because
that is the kind of figure that gets quoted for years.

**On mobile internet adoption, the arithmetic is right and the labelling is not.** The brief states, citing GSMA,
that unique mobile internet users were about 21% of the population in 2025, and builds its central argument on it.
Our own figure is 11,193,584 mobile internet subscriptions at June 2026, or 77.6 per 100 inhabitants, with 74.35 per
100 inhabitants published as a key indicator in our Q4 2025 report. The SDS confirms that both are correct on their
own definitions and that the gap between subscriptions and unique users is real. The fault is that the brief does
not say which it is measuring, so two figures three and a half times apart sit in the same document — and the one
carrying RURA's name is the one that will be questioned.

**Household electricity access is slightly overstated and misdated.** REG puts household access at **84.6% as of
July 2025**. The brief gives a range of 85% to 88% and presents it as the 2026 position. The true figure sits below
the floor of that range, and a July 2025 figure labelled 2026 overstates how current the brief is. The brief's grid
and off-grid split — 60% and 25% of households — remains unsourced, and its own two components sum to 85%, which
leaves its 88% upper bound unsupported by its own arithmetic.

**The brief also misses the market development of the year.** It leads on an unsourced report that a third mobile
licence is under consideration, and omits Mobile Number Portability entirely. MNP is the change actually under
way: operator service centres were inspected and found ready at six locations, a public awareness campaign was
delivered, and implementation falls in FY 2026/27. The brief's regulatory section likewise names neither of the two
regulations made during the year, on infrastructure sharing and on quality of service.

**What the report would add.** Mobile penetration crossed 100 subscriptions per 100 inhabitants, from 95.1 to
100.6. 4G became the majority of mobile internet at 56.8%, up from 50.9%, on 6.31 million subscriptions. Equipped
international bandwidth rose 58.7% and bandwidth used 22.0%. Mobile money reached 61.8 SIM cards per 100
inhabitants, on 8.1 million linked SIM cards. None of these appears in the brief.

**One assurance worth recording.** Cross-checking the Annual Report against the four published 2025 quarterly
reports, the ICT series reconciles throughout — subscriptions, penetration, market share, the 4G share and
bandwidth all sit on the same trajectory, allowing for the different reference periods. The brief's one solidly
correct figure, MTN at about 66%, is confirmed twice over: 66.3% at December 2025 and 65.8% at June 2026. The
decline in fixed broadband is likewise confirmed in both publications, which is why the brief's reference to
continued broadband expansion is wrong on our own published evidence.

**We recommend** that the Authority does not endorse the brief in its present form, and that the author is given
the three corrections we can now evidence: 3G coverage 96.53% and 4G 98.69% at June 2026, in that order; household
electricity access 84.6% at July 2025, sourced to REG; and the adoption figure labelled with its unit. Alongside
these we would offer our verified ICT indicator set, with prior-year comparators and stated reference dates, so
that any revised version rests on RURA figures rather than on unattributed ones. Population and economic growth
should be referred to NISR and MINECOFIN, and the grid and off-grid split to REG.

The team can supply the indicator set today and can review a revised draft at short notice.

Kind regards,

Alain
Data Analytics Team

## Sender's note (not part of the email)

- **Lead with the transposed coverage figures.** They are the cleanest finding in the review: a definite error, in
  the brief's own headline table, that we can correct from our own records. It also demonstrates that the review
  was substantive rather than a sign-off.
- **The 21% figure is the one with the longest tail.** If the brief circulates with it and someone sets it beside
  our published statistics, the Authority has a visible contradiction on the same subject in the same month.
- **Do not let the coverage figures out as a national number yet.** They are an average of three operators'
  self-reported footprints, which is not the same as the share of the population covered by at least one operator,
  and KTRN is a 4G wholesaler sitting in a 3G average. Fine for correcting the brief's ordering; not yet fine for a
  published national statistic. §2 of the attachment sets out both questions for ICT.
- **There is a standing gap worth fixing.** We collect coverage every quarter, for 2G through 5G, and publish none
  of it — which is precisely why an outside author reached for GSMA instead of us, and why the DG had to ask the
  department before the Authority could answer a question about its own sector. §9 of the attachment recommends
  restoring a coverage table to the quarterly publication and summarising it in the Annual Report. The data is
  already collected and validated, so the cost is editorial.
- **Three small errors in our own text** surfaced during this check and are listed in §5 of the attachment: a stray
  percent sign on the fixed broadband rate, 9.9% against 9.8% for internet subscriptions, and 14% against 13.5% for
  the electricity customer base. None changes a conclusion; all should be fixed before printing.
- The brief's electricity numbers do not reconcile with each other — 60% grid plus 25% off-grid is 85%, the floor of
  its own 85–88% band. Mentioned in the attachment, left out of the email to keep it short.
