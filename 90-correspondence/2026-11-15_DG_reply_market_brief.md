**Subject:** Review of the Rwanda Telecommunications Market Brief — findings and recommendation

Dear Director General,

We have reviewed the market brief against the FY 2025/26 Annual Report and against our published ICT Sector
Statistics Reports for all four quarters of 2025. The full check is attached; the position in short is below.

**The brief is not fabricated, but much of it is not ours to confirm.** Of its 22 checkable claims, six are
confirmed by our publications, seven are indicators RURA does not publish at all, two concern network coverage
which we collect but no longer publish, three are materially misleading as written, and four are out of date. The
figures most likely to be quoted — network coverage, electricity access, population and economic growth — are the
ones we cannot stand behind, because they belong to GSMA, the World Bank, NISR and EDCL rather than to the
Authority. If RURA is recorded as having reviewed and accepted the brief, we adopt those numbers as regulatory
data.

**On network coverage specifically.** The brief states 3G coverage of about 99% and 4G of about 96%. We cannot
confirm either from anything RURA has published: the quarterly statistics series carried a coverage table in 2017
but no longer does, and the Annual Report has never had one. The Authority does, however, collect geographic and
population coverage from every operator every quarter, for 2G through 5G. The ICT department can settle both
figures from the Q4 FY 2025/26 operator returns today, and I would recommend asking them to do so before any
further response is given.

**One figure should be corrected before the brief circulates further.** The brief states, citing GSMA, that unique
mobile internet users were about 21% of the population in 2025, and builds its central argument on it. RURA's own
published figure for internet subscriptions is **74.35 per 100 inhabitants** as at December 2025, carried as a key
indicator in our Q4 2025 statistics report. Both are defensible on their own definitions — unique individuals
against active subscriptions — but the brief does not say so, and the two are three and a half times apart. A
reader will conclude that one of them is wrong, and the one carrying RURA's name is the one that will be
questioned.

**The brief also misses the market development of the year.** It leads on an unsourced report that a third mobile
licence is under consideration, and omits Mobile Number Portability entirely. MNP is the change actually under
way: operator service centres were inspected and found ready at six locations, a public awareness campaign was
delivered, and implementation falls in FY 2026/27. The brief's regulatory section likewise names neither of the two
regulations made during the year, on infrastructure sharing and on quality of service.

**What the report would add.** Mobile penetration crossed 100 subscriptions per 100 inhabitants, from 95.1 to
100.6. 4G became the majority of mobile internet at 56.8%, up from 50.9%, on 6.31 million subscriptions. Equipped
international bandwidth rose 58.7% and bandwidth used 22.0%. Mobile money reached 61.8 SIM cards per 100
inhabitants, on 8.1 million linked SIM cards. None of these appears in the brief.

**One assurance worth recording.** Cross-checking the Annual Report against the four published 2025 quarterly
reports, the ICT series reconciles throughout — subscriptions, penetration, market share, the 4G share and
bandwidth all sit on the same trajectory, allowing for the different reference periods. The brief's one solidly
correct figure, MTN at about 66%, is confirmed twice over: 66.3% at December 2025 and 65.8% at June 2026. The
decline in fixed broadband is likewise confirmed in both publications, which is why the brief's reference to
continued broadband expansion is wrong on our own published evidence.

**We recommend** that the Authority does not endorse the brief in its present form; that the adoption figure is
corrected; and that we offer the author our verified ICT indicator set, with prior-year comparators and stated
reference dates, so that any revised version rests on published RURA figures. The population, economic growth and
electricity access claims should be referred to NISR, MINECOFIN and EDCL respectively.

The team can supply the verified indicator set today, and can review a revised draft at short notice.

Kind regards,

Alain
Data Analytics Team

## Sender's note (not part of the email)

- **The 21% figure is the one that matters.** Everything else is housekeeping. If the brief circulates with it and
  someone sets it beside the Annual Report, the Authority has a visible contradiction on the same subject in the
  same month.
- **Be ready for "so is 99% coverage right or not?"** The honest answer is that no RURA publication reports
  coverage any more — the quarterly series dropped the table it had in 2017 — so we cannot say from anything
  published. We do collect it from operators quarterly, including 5G, so ICT can answer from the returns. For
  context only, and not for quoting: in June 2017 3G population coverage was 93.11% and 4G 64.30%.
- **There is a standing gap here worth fixing.** We collect coverage every quarter and publish none of it, which is
  precisely why an outside author reached for GSMA instead of us. §9 of the attachment recommends restoring a
  coverage table to the quarterly publication and summarising it in the Annual Report. The data is already
  collected and validated, so the cost is editorial.
- **Three small errors in our own text** surfaced during this check and are listed in §5 of the attachment: a stray
  percent sign on the fixed broadband rate, 9.9% against 9.8% for internet subscriptions, and 14% against 13.5% for
  the electricity customer base. None changes a conclusion; all should be fixed before printing.
- The brief's electricity numbers do not reconcile with each other — 60% grid plus 25% off-grid is 85%, the floor of
  its own 85–88% band. Mentioned in the attachment, left out of the email to keep it short.
