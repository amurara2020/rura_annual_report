# Email to the Director General — with annex

**Subject:** Review of the Rwanda Telecommunications Market Brief — not recommended for endorsement

*Alternative subjects, depending on who may see it onward:*
- *Neutral, safe to forward to the author:* Review of the Rwanda Telecommunications Market Brief — findings and corrections
- *If the DG wants the scale up front:* Market brief review — 6 of 22 claims confirmed; two figures to correct

---

Dear Director General,

We have reviewed the market brief against the Annual Report FY 2025/26 and the six published ICT Sector Statistics
Reports from Q1 2025 to Q2 2026. The claim-by-claim comparison is annexed.

**Conclusion: the data is not reliable enough for the Authority to endorse.** Of 22 checkable claims, only **6 are
confirmed** by RURA publications. **2 are wrong**, **9 are misleading, unsourced or out of date**, and **5 are
figures RURA does not produce**.

The brief is a secondary compilation rather than a data document. Most of its quantitative content comes from GSMA,
the World Bank, NISR and REG rather than from RURA — and in the one place it reports our own sector's headline
numbers, network coverage, it has them the wrong way round.

Its overall picture is broadly right in direction: extensive network coverage, strong government commitment to ICT,
and a real gap between network availability and usage. The numbers supporting that picture are not dependable, and
several are out of date.

**Our advice is that the Authority should not endorse the brief, and should not allow it to be cited as reflecting
RURA data.** If the author corrects and re-sources it, it would be a reasonable overview.

**Two findings matter most.**

The brief's coverage table has its two figures transposed. It reports 3G population coverage at about 99% and 4G at
about 96%; the position reported by the ICT department is **3G at 96.53% and 4G at 98.69%**. As written, the brief
tells readers that 3G reaches more of the population than 4G, which reverses both the position and the year's
principal ICT finding — 4G rising to 56.8% of mobile internet subscriptions.

The brief's central argument rests on a GSMA estimate that unique mobile internet users are about 21% of the
population. RURA does not measure internet users; we count connection points, and our indicator framework assigns
no collection source to a unique-user figure. Our published position is 77.6 internet subscriptions per 100
inhabitants at June 2026. The estimate also does not reconcile with our data: 21% would require every mobile
internet user to hold **3.7 active subscriptions**. The brief cites no GSMA edition, so its definition cannot be
checked.

On household electricity access, the brief's range of 85–88% may well be correct. The most recent figure we hold is
**84.6% (REG, July 2025)**, and our own grid connections rose **13.5%** over FY 2025/26, from 1,768,830 to
2,008,342, so access has very likely risen since. The fault is that the brief gives no source and no date for a
figure it presents as the 2026 position. **We will confirm the current figure with REG** rather than treat the range
as an error.

**One caution on the coverage figures.** They are not published anywhere. Coverage is absent from all six quarterly
reports and from the Annual Report, and the figures we hold are understood to come from the Q3 2026 report still in
preparation. They are sound for correcting the brief internally, but should not be cited externally until that
report is published, and their reference date should be confirmed.

**One matter in our own reporting, which I would rather raise now.** Cross-checking against the Q2 2026 statistics
report — which covers 1 April to 30 June 2026, the Annual Report's own reference date — every ICT figure matches
exactly, with one exception. Used international internet bandwidth appears in the Annual Report as 283,607 to
345,864 Mbps, a rise of 22.0%; the published Q2 2026 report gives 285.7 to 379.9 Gbps, a rise of 33.0%. The Q1 2026
report records 351,015 Mbps at March 2026, which is above the Annual Report's June figure and would imply a fall in
the final quarter. The Annual Report figure appears in three places — the at-a-glance card, the narrative and the
statistical annex — and I recommend ICT confirms the correct figure and that all three are corrected before
printing. It affects no conclusion in this review: bandwidth grew on either set of figures.

Every other indicator we checked — subscriptions, penetration, market share, fixed telephony, Pay TV and equipped
bandwidth — matches the published quarterly figures exactly.

**We recommend** that the Authority does not endorse the brief in its present form; that the author is given the
coverage correction above; that the GSMA figure is properly sourced and not presented as comparable with ours; and
that the electricity, population and economic growth figures are sourced and dated, with the current access figure
confirmed by REG.

**One standing matter.** We collect network coverage from every operator each quarter, for 2G through 5G, and have
published none of it in six consecutive quarters. That is why an outside author used third-party estimates rather
than our figures, and why answering a question about coverage in our own sector required unpublished records. I
recommend restoring a coverage table to the quarterly publication and summarising it in the Annual Report. The data
is already collected and validated, so the cost is editorial.

The team can supply our verified indicator set today and review a revised draft at short notice.

Kind regards,

Alain
Data Analytics Team

## Assurance on our own figures

The Annual Report's ICT series was cross-checked against the published quarterly reports, including **Q2 2026,
which covers 1 April to 30 June 2026 — the Annual Report's own reference date**. This is a direct comparison, not an
approximate one.

|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile-cellular subscriptions | 14,501,766 | 14,501,766 | **Exact** |
| Mobile subscriptions per 100 inhabitants | 100.58 | 100.6 | **Exact** |
| Internet subscriptions | 11,193,584 | 11,193,584 | **Exact** |
| Internet subscriptions per 100 inhabitants | 77.6 | — | n/a |
| MTN / Airtel market share | 65.8% / 34.2% | 65.8% / 34.2% | **Exact** |

| Active Pay TV subscribers | 193,312 | 193,312 | **Exact** |
| Equipped international bandwidth | 1,197.4 Gbps | 1,197,355 Mbps | **Exact** |
| **Used international bandwidth** | **379.9 Gbps (▲33.0%)** | **345,864 Mbps (▲22.0%)** | **Does not agree** |

**One discrepancy requires attention in our own reporting.** Every ICT figure in the Annual Report matches the
published Q2 2026 report exactly, with one exception: **used international internet bandwidth**. The Annual Report
gives 283,607 → 345,864 Mbps (▲22.0%) in its at-a-glance card, its narrative and its statistical annex; the
published Q2 2026 report gives 285.7 → 379.9 Gbps (▲33.0%) for the same period.

The Annual Report figure also sits below the previous quarter's published value: the Q1 2026 report records used
bandwidth of 351,015 Mbps at March 2026, which would imply a fall over the final quarter, where the Q2 2026 report
records a rise to 379.9 Gbps.

This should be resolved by the ICT department and the Annual Report corrected before printing. It does not affect
any conclusion in this review — the brief's claim that bandwidth is growing is correct either way, and understated
on both sets of figures.
