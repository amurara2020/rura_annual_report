## 2. The approach we have taken

The report has been rebuilt rather than updated, around four principles.

**One structure across all sectors.** Every sector is now reported under the same five headings — Sector Profile, Legal and Regulatory Framework, Licensing, Market Performance, and Compliance Monitoring and Enforcement — so the five sectors can be read and compared on the same terms. Content that did not belong to a sector was moved to the chapter that covers it: consumer complaints to Consumer Protection, challenges to the risk chapter, priorities to the outlook chapter.

**From activity to outcome.** The previous report largely counted what the Authority did. This one asks what changed as a result. Where the evidence allows, a finding is reported as: the regulatory issue, the evidence, RURA's intervention, and the observable outcome. The Kigali Gross Contract Model is the clearest example — daily ridership rose from about 190,000 to 219,000, corridor travel times fell 43%, and peak waiting intervals halved from 12.6 to 6.1 minutes.

**Every figure carries its comparison.** Headline indicators now show the current value, the prior-year value, the change and whether that change is an improvement. This applies to results that moved the wrong way as well: electricity grid losses rose to 16.77%, recorded outages to 1,917, and liquid fuel storage stands at about a third of the national target. These are presented as regulatory concerns rather than omitted.

**Figures are verified against their source, not carried forward.** Every reported number has been checked against the table it comes from, and every calculated value is marked and its formula recorded. This is what surfaced the discrepancies now being resolved with departments — for example, the nuclear licensing revenue performance rate, which has been corrected, and the tower count, which has been reconciled.

Alongside the report, the team has produced a **Visual Design Standard** so that charts, tables, indicator cards and status markers follow one system across the document and in future years, rather than being formatted page by page.
