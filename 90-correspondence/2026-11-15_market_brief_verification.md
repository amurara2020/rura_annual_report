# Verification of the "Rwanda Telecommunications Market Brief"

**Requested by:** Director General, 15 November 2026 — *"review with your team and advise if data used is correct"*
**Source of the brief:** shared with the DG by a third party; the DG notes it was drafted with ChatGPT
**Checked against:** RURA Annual Report FY 2025/26 (management-agreed version), ICT and Energy chapters, FY 2025/26 in Numbers, and the statistical annex
**Prepared by:** Data Analytics Team

---

## Summary

The brief contains **22 checkable factual claims**. Of these:

| | Claims |
|---|---|
| Confirmed by the Annual Report | 6 |
| Not in the Annual Report — RURA does not publish the indicator | 9 |
| Materially misleading as written | 3 |
| Out of date or contradicted in direction | 4 |

Nothing in the brief is fabricated outright. The problem is different and more awkward: **the brief's most quotable
numbers are the ones RURA cannot stand behind**, because they are not RURA indicators at all. If the Authority is
seen to have reviewed and accepted the brief, it adopts figures from GSMA, the World Bank and unattributed sources
as though they were regulatory data.

**One claim should be corrected before the brief circulates further** — the mobile internet adoption figure
(item 11), which sits 57 percentage points away from RURA's own published measure of the same subject.

## 1. Confirmed by the Annual Report

| # | Claim in the brief | Report evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two licensed mobile network operators | MTN Rwandacell Plc and Airtel Rwanda Ltd (§2.1.4) | **Correct** |
| 2 | MTN market leader, approximately 66% share | 65.8% at June 2026, up from 64.0% at June 2025 | **Correct** — but undated in the brief, and see note below |
| 3 | Airtel the second operator | 34.2% at June 2026, down from 36.0% | **Correct** |
| 4 | RURA responsible for licensing, spectrum and oversight | §2.1.2 Regulatory Environment | **Correct** |
| 5 | Growing international bandwidth capacity | Equipped 754,578 → 1,197,355 Mbps (▲58.7%); used 283,607 → 345,864 Mbps (▲22.0%) | **Correct and understated** — the brief gives no figures |
| 6 | Multiple regional fibre routes to submarine cables | Fibre backbone, access and metro tables by operator (Table 10); infrastructure-sharing regulation 2026 | **Consistent** |

**Note on item 2.** The brief gives one market-share figure. The report publishes two, for different services:
mobile-cellular **65.8%** and mobile internet **65.0%**. A brief quoting "66%" without saying which service, and
as at which date, invites exactly the question the DG is being asked.

## 2. Not in the Annual Report — we cannot confirm or deny

These are not RURA indicators. The report contains no figure for any of them, so the Authority has no basis to
validate them. They would have to be verified with their own source holders.

| # | Claim in the brief | Where it would have to come from |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 3G population coverage approximately 99% | ICT department QoS and coverage records — **not** the Annual Report |
| 8 | 4G population coverage approximately 96% | As above |
| 9 | Population approximately 14 million; 445 people per km² | NISR |
| 10 | Economy grew 9.4% in 2025 and 9.7% in H1 2026 | NISR / MINECOFIN |
| 12 | More than one million additional mobile internet users by 2031 | GSMA projection |
| 13 | Household electricity access approximately 85–88% | EDCL / MININFRA |
| 14 | Grid connections approximately 60% of households | EDCL / MININFRA |
| 15 | Off-grid access approximately 25% of households | EDCL / MININFRA |
| 16 | A third MNO licence is reportedly under consideration | Unattributed in the brief; nothing in the report |

**On coverage (items 7–8).** The report publishes no population-coverage percentages for any technology. Its
nearest content is the tower count — **1,779 towers in total, of which only 13 were built during FY 2025/26** — and
a single in-building solution at BK Arena. If the DG is asked to confirm 99% and 96%, those figures must come from
the ICT department's own monitoring, and they should be dated.

**On electricity (items 13–15).** The report reports **customer connections** — 2,008,342, up 13.5% from
1,768,830 — and no access rate at all. Separately, the brief's own three electricity numbers do not reconcile:
60% grid plus 25% off-grid is 85%, which is the floor of its own "85–88%" band, so the 88% upper bound is
inconsistent with the components the brief itself gives.

**On a third licence (item 16).** See §4 item 20 — this is the brief's most significant omission, not just an
unverifiable claim.

## 3. Materially misleading as written

### 11. "Unique mobile internet users represented approximately 21% of the population in 2025" (GSMA)

**This is the item to correct.** The figure may well be accurate on GSMA's own definition — unique *individuals*
using mobile internet. But it is placed in a brief about Rwanda's telecommunications performance with no indication
that it measures something different from what the regulator publishes, and the brief then builds its central
argument on it ("digital adoption remains below infrastructure readiness levels").

RURA's published measure of the same subject, in the report now with the DG:

- Active mobile internet subscriptions: **11,193,584** at June 2026, up 9.82%
- Against the report's own population denominator (~14.42 million, implied by 14,501,766 subscriptions at 100.6
  per 100 inhabitants): **77.7 mobile internet subscriptions per 100 inhabitants**

21% and 77.7% are both defensible on their own definitions, and the gap between them is the well-known one between
unique users and active SIMs. But as the brief is written, a reader will conclude that one of the two is wrong —
and the one carrying RURA's name is the one that will be questioned.

**Recommendation:** if the GSMA figure is retained, it must state that it counts unique individuals, name the GSMA
edition and year, and show RURA's subscription figure alongside it.

### 17. "Commercial 5G availability — available in parts of Kigali through MTN Rwanda"

The report states only that **"5G services commenced"** — one sentence, in the overview. There is no operator
attribution, no geographic detail, and no subscriber figure anywhere in the report; the mobile internet technology
chart (Figure 17) covers **4G, 3G and EDGE (2G) only**. The attribution to MTN and to Kigali may be correct, but it
is not ours to confirm, and the report cannot be cited for it.

### 18. "Continued broadband expansion"

Directionally wrong for fixed broadband, which is the segment the sentence sits next to. The report records fixed
broadband at **0.584 subscriptions per 100 inhabitants** and **declining** over the year. Mobile broadband did
expand; fixed did not. The brief does not distinguish them.

## 4. Out of date, or missing what the report would add

| # | Issue | What the report says |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | Brief asserts "financial inclusion" ambition with no evidence | Mobile money penetration **61.8 SIM cards per 100 inhabitants**, up from 55.0 — a 6.8-point gain in one year |
| 20 | Brief leads on an unsourced third-licence rumour and **omits Mobile Number Portability entirely** | MNP is the actual market-structure change of the year: service centres inspected and found ready at six locations (Nyabugogo, CHIC, UTC, Remera, Giporoso, Kicukiro), a public awareness campaign delivered, and implementation flagged for FY 2026/27 |
| 21 | Brief's "Spectrum and Regulatory Framework" section names no instrument | **Two regulations were made during the year**: No. 20/R/ICT/RURA/2026 on telecommunication infrastructure sharing, and No. 21/R/ICT/RURA/2026 on mobile and fixed network quality of service. A brief on the regulatory framework that omits both is out of date |
| 22 | Brief: "increasing investment in digital infrastructure" | Needs nuance: valid licences and authorizations **fell 11%**, from 1,684 to 1,499, and tower authorizations fell from **169 to 13**. The report explains this as a shift from build-out to optimisation of an established base — a more defensible story than "increasing investment", and a better one |

The brief also misses the two headline ICT results of the year entirely: **mobile penetration crossed 100
subscriptions per 100 inhabitants** (95.1 → 100.6), and **4G became the majority of mobile internet** at 56.8%, up
from 50.9%, on 6.31 million 4G subscriptions (▲22.8%).

## 5. Three items in our own report to tidy (found during this check)

Raised here so they are fixed in our text, not because the brief is wrong about them:

1. §2.1.4.6 reads "Fixed Broadband Subscriptions per 100 Inhabitants stood at **0.584%**" — the figure is a rate per
   100 inhabitants, not a percentage. Remove the percent sign.
2. The overview gives internet subscriptions as "**9.9%** from 10.1 to 11.1 million"; the at-a-glance card and
   §2.1.4.6 give **9.8%** from 10.19 to 11.19 million. Standardise on 9.82%.
3. The Outlook says the electricity customer base "expanded by **14%**"; the figure throughout the Energy chapter is
   **13.5%**.

---

## 6. Recommendation to the DG

1. **Do not endorse the brief as it stands.** Roughly half its numbers are not RURA indicators, and the Authority
   would be adopting third-party figures as regulatory data.
2. **Correct item 11 before it circulates further.** The 21% adoption figure, as presented, contradicts RURA's own
   published position on the same subject.
3. **Offer the author our verified ICT figures** — the FY 2025/26 at-a-glance set, with prior-year comparators and
   stated reference dates. Every figure in §1 and §3 above is already published and sourced.
4. **Correct the regulatory section**, which omits both regulations made during the year and substitutes an
   unsourced third-licence rumour for the MNP programme that is actually under way.
5. Refer the population, GDP and electricity-access claims to **NISR**, **MINECOFIN** and **EDCL/MININFRA**
   respectively. They are not ours to confirm.
