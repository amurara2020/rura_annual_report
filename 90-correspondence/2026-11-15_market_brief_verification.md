# Verification of the "Rwanda Telecommunications Market Brief"

**Requested by:** Director General, 15 November 2026 — *"review with your team and advise if data used is correct"*
**Source of the brief:** shared with the DG by a third party; the DG notes it was drafted with ChatGPT
**Checked against:** RURA Annual Report FY 2025/26 (management-agreed version), ICT and Energy chapters, FY 2025/26
in Numbers and the statistical annex; the published ICT Sector Statistics Reports for Q1–Q4 2025; the 2017 quarterly
telecom statistics report; and RURA's standing mobile network operator return form
**Prepared by:** Data Analytics Team

---

## Summary

The brief contains **22 checkable factual claims**. Of these:

| | Claims |
|---|---|
| Confirmed by the Annual Report | 6 |
| Published by RURA, but in the quarterly statistics series — not the Annual Report | 2 |
| Not RURA indicators at all | 7 |
| Materially misleading as written | 3 |
| Out of date or contradicted in direction | 4 |

Nothing in the brief is fabricated outright. The problem is different and more awkward: **the brief's most quotable
numbers are the ones RURA cannot stand behind**, because they are not RURA indicators at all. If the Authority is
seen to have reviewed and accepted the brief, it adopts figures from GSMA, the World Bank and unattributed sources
as though they were regulatory data.

**One claim should be corrected before the brief circulates further** — the mobile internet adoption figure
(item 11), which sits 57 percentage points away from RURA's own published measure of the same subject.

## 2. Network coverage — RURA collects it, but no longer publishes it

**Correction to an earlier draft of this note.** We first recorded coverage as a RURA-published indicator, on the
strength of the older quarterly series. Having now read all four 2025 quarterly reports, that is wrong and the
position is narrower:

- The **2017** quarterly report carried a standing table, *Mobile network coverage per type of technology*,
  reporting geographic and population coverage for each generation.
- The **current series does not.** The ICT Sector Statistics Reports for Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q4 of 2025 contain **no
  coverage section at all** — the word "coverage" does not appear in any of the four, and neither does 5G.
- The Annual Report carries none either.

So **no current RURA publication reports network coverage**, and the brief's figures cannot be checked against
anything we have published.

**But the Authority does hold the data.** The standing operator return ("Mobile Network Operator Form", section 3,
*Mobile network Coverage*) requires every MNO to report, each quarter, geographic coverage (%) and population
coverage (%) for **2G, 3G, 4G and 5G**, plus base stations by technology and by province. The ICT department can
therefore confirm or refute items 7 and 8 from the operator returns within the day.

| # | Claim in the brief | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 3G population coverage approximately 99% | **Not published by RURA. Verifiable from operator returns.** The last figure we hold is from June 2017: 3G population coverage 93.11%, geographic 76.27% |
| 8 | 4G population coverage approximately 96% | **Not published by RURA. Verifiable from operator returns.** June 2017: 4G LTE population coverage 64.30%, geographic 51.40% |

The brief uses the right metric name — *population* coverage is RURA's own term, and the higher of the two measures.
Anyone quoting a coverage figure should say which of the two it is, since geographic coverage is materially lower
for every generation.

**The 2017 comparators are given only to show order of magnitude and direction of travel. They are nine years old
and must not be published or quoted.**

## 4. Materially misleading as written

### 11. "Unique mobile internet users represented approximately 21% of the population in 2025" (GSMA)

**This is the item to correct.** The figure may well be accurate on GSMA's own definition — unique *individuals*
using mobile internet. But it is placed in a brief about Rwanda's telecommunications performance with no indication
that it measures something different from what the regulator publishes, and the brief then builds its central
argument on it ("digital adoption remains below infrastructure readiness levels").

RURA's published measure of the same subject:

- **Internet subscriptions per 100 inhabitants: 74.35** at December 2025 — published, as a standing key indicator,
  in the ICT Sector Statistics Report for Q4 2025 (Table 1)
- Active mobile internet subscriptions **11,193,584** at June 2026, up 9.82% (Annual Report)

So the Authority's own published adoption figure is **74.35 per 100 inhabitants**, against the brief's 21%. Both are
defensible on their own definitions — the gap is the well-known one between unique individuals and active
subscriptions — but the brief does not say so, and the two numbers are three and a half times apart. A reader will
conclude that one of them is wrong, and the one carrying RURA's name is the one that will be questioned.

**Recommendation:** if the GSMA figure is retained, it must state that it counts unique individuals, name the GSMA
edition and year, and show RURA's subscription figure alongside it.

### 17. "Commercial 5G availability — available in parts of Kigali through MTN Rwanda"

The report states only that **"5G services commenced"** — one sentence, in the overview. There is no operator
attribution, no geographic detail, and no subscriber figure anywhere in the report; the mobile internet technology
chart (Figure 17) covers **4G, 3G and EDGE (2G) only**. The attribution to MTN and to Kigali may be correct, but it
is not ours to confirm, and the report cannot be cited for it.

### 18. "Continued broadband expansion"

Directionally wrong for fixed broadband, which is the segment the sentence sits next to. The report records fixed
broadband at **0.584 subscriptions per 100 inhabitants** and **declining** over the year. Mobile broadband did
expand; fixed did not. The brief does not distinguish them.

## 6. Cross-check against the published quarterly statistics

The four 2025 quarterly reports let us check the Annual Report's ICT series against separately published figures.
**Everything reconciles.** The two publications use different reference periods — the quarterly series is calendar
quarters, the Annual Report is June to June — so the figures are not identical, and should not be expected to be.

| Indicator | Published Q4 2025 (31 Dec 2025) | Annual Report (30 Jun 2026) | Consistent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile-cellular subscriptions | 13,685,892 | 14,501,766 | Yes |
| Mobile subscriptions per 100 inhabitants | 97.03 | 100.6 | Yes — crosses 100 in H2 |
| MTN market share | 66.3% | 65.8% | Yes |
| Airtel market share | 33.7% | 34.2% | Yes |
| Internet subscriptions | 10,487,460 | 11,193,584 | Yes |
| Internet subscriptions per 100 inhabitants | 74.35 | — (not stated) | n/a |
| 4G share of mobile internet | 56.0% (5,823,690 of 10,401,239) | 56.8% | Yes |
| Used international bandwidth | 344,907 Mbps | 345,864 Mbps | Yes |
| Fixed broadband per 100 inhabitants | 0.611 | 0.584 | Yes — **confirms the decline** |

Two points worth drawing out:

- **The fixed broadband decline is corroborated.** The rate fell from 0.611 per 100 inhabitants at December 2025 to
  0.584 at June 2026, in two separately published sources. The brief's "continued broadband expansion" is wrong on
  our own published evidence, not merely on the Annual Report's.
- **The brief's 66% market share is confirmed twice** — 66.3% at December 2025 and 65.8% at June 2026. This is the
  one figure in the brief we can stand behind without qualification, beyond saying which date it refers to.

The quarterly series also supports a point the brief misses entirely: **mobile money linked SIM cards reached
8,135,275 at December 2025, up 11.5% year on year**, with 193,810 active agent SIMs. The brief asserts a financial
inclusion agenda in its opening paragraph and offers no evidence for it.

## 7. Three items in our own report to tidy (found during this check)

Raised here so they are fixed in our text, not because the brief is wrong about them:

1. §2.1.4.6 reads "Fixed Broadband Subscriptions per 100 Inhabitants stood at **0.584%**" — a rate per 100
   inhabitants written with a percent sign. The quarterly reports do the same ("0.611%" at December 2025), so this
   is a house convention rather than a one-off typo. It is still wrong, and fixing it is a decision for both
   publications, not just this one.
2. The overview gives internet subscriptions as "**9.9%** from 10.1 to 11.1 million"; the at-a-glance card and
   §2.1.4.6 give **9.8%** from 10.19 to 11.19 million. Standardise on 9.82%.
3. The Outlook says the electricity customer base "expanded by **14%**"; the figure throughout the Energy chapter is
   **13.5%**.

## 8. Recommendation to the DG

1. **Do not endorse the brief as it stands.** A third of its numbers are not RURA indicators at all, and the
   Authority would be adopting third-party figures as regulatory data.
2. **The two coverage figures can be settled today, but only internally.** No RURA publication reports coverage
   any more, so ask the ICT department to confirm 3G and 4G population coverage from the Q4 FY2025/26 operator
   returns. Until they do, the Authority cannot endorse 99% and 96%.
3. **Correct item 11 before it circulates further.** The 21% adoption figure, as presented, contradicts RURA's own
   published position on the same subject.
4. **Offer the author our verified ICT figures** — the FY 2025/26 at-a-glance set, with prior-year comparators and
   stated reference dates. Every figure in §1 and §3 above is already published and sourced.
5. **Correct the regulatory section**, which omits both regulations made during the year and substitutes an
   unsourced third-licence rumour for the MNP programme that is actually under way.
6. Refer the population, GDP and electricity-access claims to **NISR**, **MINECOFIN** and **EDCL/MININFRA**
   respectively. They are not ours to confirm.

## 9. A gap worth raising separately

RURA collects network coverage from every operator every quarter, for 2G, 3G, 4G and 5G, on both a geographic and
a population basis — and publishes none of it. The quarterly series carried a coverage table in 2017 and has since
dropped it; the Annual Report never had one.

That is why a third party writing about Rwanda's coverage reached for GSMA and unattributed sources instead of our
own numbers, and why the Authority cannot now confirm a claim about its own sector. The same applies to 5G: the
Annual Report says only that "5G services commenced", and the quarterly series does not mention it at all, although
the operator return has collected 5G coverage fields throughout.

**Recommendation:** restore a coverage table — geographic and population, by generation, including 5G — to the
quarterly publication, and carry a summary of it in the Annual Report. The data is already being collected and
validated every quarter, so the cost is editorial, not analytical.
