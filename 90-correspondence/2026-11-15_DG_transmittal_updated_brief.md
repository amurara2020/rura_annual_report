# Email to the DG — transmitting the updated market brief

**Subject:** Rwanda Telecommunications Market Brief — updated version attached
**Attach:** `Rwanda_Telecommunications_Market_Brief_UPDATED.docx`

---

Dear Director General,

As instructed, we have updated the market brief rather than returning it for correction. The revised brief is
attached, issued in RURA's name, with every figure sourced and dated.

**What was corrected.** The brief's two network coverage figures were transposed: it reported 3G at about 99% and
4G at about 96%, where the position is **3G at 96.53% and 4G at 98.69%**. It described broadband as expanding,
where fixed broadband in fact declined over the year. It measured digital adoption using a third-party estimate of
unique users without noting that RURA reports subscriptions, which is a different quantity and not convertible into
it. And its regulatory section named no instrument made during the year and did not mention Mobile Number
Portability, while presenting a third mobile licence as the coming change in market structure.

All of these are corrected, and the year's actual results are now included: mobile penetration passing one
subscription per inhabitant, 4G becoming the majority mobile internet technology at 56.8%, international bandwidth
in use rising 33.0%, and mobile money reaching 61.8 linked SIM cards per 100 inhabitants.

Figures the Authority does not produce — population density and economic growth — are referred to NISR and
MINECOFIN rather than reproduced, so that RURA is not seen to vouch for them.

**Two matters before it circulates.**

The network coverage figures are **provisional**. They come from the ICT Sector Statistics Report for Q3 2026, which
is still in preparation, and are marked as such in the brief. Once this document is shared they are effectively in
the public domain ahead of that report, so I recommend ICT confirms the same figures will be carried when it
publishes.

The brief states used international bandwidth at **379.9 Gbps**, taken from the published Q2 2026 statistics
report. The **Annual Report gives 345,864 Mbps for the same period**. Both documents may reach the same reader, and
I recommend the Annual Report figure is verified and corrected before printing.

**One item remains open.** Household electricity access is given as 84.6% at July 2025, the latest figure from REG.
We have asked REG for the current position and will update the brief if it arrives before you send it.

The full claim-by-claim review of the original brief is available should you need the detail behind any of the
above.

Kind regards,

Alain
Data Analytics Team

## Sender's note (not part of the email)

- **The coverage decision is the one to be conscious of.** You asked us to use the figures and we have, marked
  provisional and sourced to the Q3 2026 report in preparation. The risk is small but real: a figure that
  circulates before its own report publishes tends to be quoted back, and if Q3 2026 lands on a different number
  the Authority has two. A line to ICT now closes that off.
- **Expect one question on the bandwidth line.** If anyone compares the brief with the Annual Report they will find
  379.9 Gbps against 345,864 Mbps. The brief uses the published quarterly figure, which is the defensible one. The
  Annual Report is what needs fixing.
- **What was removed, if asked:** the third-licence passage, the grid and off-grid household split, population
  density, GDP growth, and the attribution of 5G to a named operator and location. Each was either unsourced or not
  ours to confirm. Nothing that could be evidenced was dropped.
- The brief can be regenerated quickly if REG returns a current access figure or Q3 2026 publishes.
