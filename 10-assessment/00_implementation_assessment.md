## 1. Headline finding

The agreed structure is sound and unusually well prepared. The baseline document has already done the hard editorial work: it applies a consistent five-heading template to every sector, it has relocated misplaced content and recorded where each block came from, it flags 22 numerical contradictions, and it distinguishes calculated values with a dagger convention. That discipline should be preserved as the house standard.

**The report's problem is not structure. It is that the evidence base is unevenly distributed, and it is thinnest exactly where regulatory credibility is strongest.**

Counting the baseline's own annotations: **25 `MISSING` notes, 37 `PARTIAL` notes, 22 `CHECK` verification items, and roughly 60 `[DATA REQUIRED]` cells.** Sorting those by what they are about produces a clear pattern:

| Type of content | State |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Market/access statistics** (subscriptions, customers, production, licences, fleet) | Strong to very strong. ICT has six-year series; water has six years; transport has full two-year comparisons |
| **Price decisions** | Referenced in four sectors, quantified in none |
| **Service quality and reliability** | Present only for Energy (and it worsened) and Transport (and it improved). Absent for ICT, Water and Nuclear |
| **Compliance outcomes** | Activity counts exist for four sectors. Outcomes — what was found, what was ordered, what was remedied — exist for none |
| **Consumer protection** | One sector of five, and its two published figures contradict each other |
| **Institutional performance** (Strategic Plan, Action Plan, finance, audit, risk) | Largely empty, blocked mostly by three internal files |

So the report currently answers *"what changed and by how much"* well for access and volume, and barely at all for price, quality, compliance and RURA's own performance. Those four are precisely the dimensions on which a regulator's annual report is judged.

## 2. Assessment against the four required categories

### A. Already available and usable

Retain substantially as drafted:

- **ICT Market Performance** — the report's analytical centre of gravity. Six-year series for mobile penetration, internet subscriptions, mobile-money SIMs, fixed telephony and pay TV; bandwidth equipped and used from 2023; market shares; full tariff tables; towers and fibre.
- **Water production and subscriptions** — six clean years (2020/21–2025/26), already indexed, and already yielding the report's best derived insight: production per subscription fell from 226 to 196 m³/year while connections grew 79%.
- **Transport fleet, licensing and the Gross Contract Model results** — full two-year comparisons plus a genuinely measured intervention outcome (ridership 190,000→219,000/day, corridor travel time −43%, peak headway 12.6→6.1 min, revenue +39% to RWF 2.32 bn).
- **Nuclear licensing and inspection geography** — 46 applications, 37 licences, 126 inspections with district-level detail for two provinces, plus the cyclotron licensing achievement.
- **Energy petroleum and LPG** — LPG storage by province, LPG imports over four years, liquid fuel storage against the 337 million litre policy target.
- **Water expansion project inspections** — 22 projects, 524,202 people served, 192 schools and 62 health facilities connected, by district. This is the only place in the draft where an inspection programme is reported with its outcomes attached, and it should be the template for the rest.
- **The Strategic Plan evidence columns** (chapter 2) — already populated for all 9 goals and all 17 initiatives; only the ratings are missing.
- **The nine-risk register evidence column** (chapter 7) — already quantified; only ratings and owners are missing.
- **The annex ICT licensing tables** — fourteen detailed tables, correctly placed in the annex.

### B. Incomplete — content exists, needs data, comparison or interpretation

- **ICT** — needs affordability (price per GB, basket as % of income), a market-concentration trend, and a 5-year tariff series.
- **Energy** — electricity appears only in the Sector Profile; there is no electricity *market* section. Needs generation mix, access rate against target, consumption per customer, SAIDI/SAIFI, and all series extended from two years to five.
- **Water** — needs non-revenue water, drinking-water quality compliance, hours of supply, access against the national target, and operator/district disaggregation.
- **Transport** — licensing and fleet statistics are good; the AFC/e-ticketing dimension is entirely unexploited (see §3).
- **Nuclear** — inspections are counted but not rated; several reported rates ("compliance rate 23%", "sector growth 110%", "performance rate 49.7%") are undefined.
- **Chapter 2** — evidence complete, ratings absent: all 9 goals and all 17 initiatives read "Not assessed".
- **Chapter 7** — nine risks evidenced, none rated or owned.
- **Chapter 8** — eleven priorities named, 33 target/deadline/owner cells empty.

### C. Genuinely missing

Stated as specific variables, not "data required":

1. **The four price decisions of the year** — electricity tariff by customer class, combined urban water and sanitation tariff by consumption block, symmetric termination rates, intercity fares: previous level, new level, % change, effective date.
2. **ICT QoS results by operator** — call drop rate, call setup success rate, data throughput, each against its licensed threshold, for both years.
3. **The entire Energy compliance section** — inspections of petrol stations, LPG facilities and electrical installations; fuel quality test results (samples taken, passed, failed by product); directives, penalties, revocations.
4. **Water NRW and drinking-water quality** — NRW % by operator and service area; samples tested and compliant by parameter and operator.
5. **Nuclear dose and incident data** — occupational dose by worker category, exceedance count, incident register, lost/orphan sources; plus inspection ratings and corrective-action closure.
6. **Complaints for ICT, Energy, Water and Nuclear** — register-level: receipt date, category, closure date, outcome, redress amount; plus the customer base per sector for normalisation.
7. **RURA's financial performance** — revenue by source, expenditure by category, approved budget vs actual, budget execution %, UAF income and disbursement, external audit opinion for five years.
8. **The M&E Framework Year-4 KPI set** — baselines, targets and actuals. One file unblocks chapter 2, annex 9.4, and headline tile 15.
9. **The Annual Action Plan end-year result** — activities planned, achieved, % achievement, main activities not achieved with reasons.
10. **The FY2024/25 commitments** — what was promised last year and what happened to each.
11. **Board composition and attendance**, plus internal audit findings and closure status.
12. **The Foreword** — text, portrait, signature.
13. **Five-year history** for energy, transport and nuclear series (currently two years at best). Without this, no 3–5 year trend chart can be produced in three of five sectors.
14. **Indicator definitions** — formula, unit, reference period, source system and data owner for every headline indicator.
15. **Transport enforcement measures by type** and accident/safety data for licensed operators.

The baseline lists 22 `CHECK` items. All must be cleared. Six are material enough to cause visible embarrassment if published:

| # | Contradiction | Why it matters |

| 1 | **Transport complaints: 1,770 received / 90% handled** (chapter 5 and Executive Summary) vs **6,716 received / 95% handled** (Transport Sector Profile) | Both figures are published in the same report; they differ by a factor of 3.8 |

The remaining sixteen — mixed March/June reference periods, fixed voice traffic totals, pay TV growth quoted as both 15.6% and 56.22%, fixed broadband −2.21% vs −2.35%, electricity customers +14% vs +13.5%, LPG imports +26.1% vs +26.2%, water licences 59 vs 63, identical March/April type-approval rows, the duplicated Gross Contract Model paragraph, the duplicated Figure 3.20 caption, the twice-described MOUs, the duplicated 2019 water tariff table, nine broken cross-references, decimal-comma inconsistency, "Gakenge"/Gakenke, and the vision/mission/values divergence from the Strategic Plan — are individually small and collectively decisive for whether the report reads as authoritative.

Two `CHECK` items are not numerical and carry real risk:

- **Photograph rights.** Sector banner images appear to come from media websites with no stated licence. Publishing a national regulator's annual report with unlicensed photography is a legal and reputational exposure. Confirm rights or replace with RURA-owned photography.
- **Vision, mission and values** differ from the Strategic Plan 2022–2027. If no formal revision exists, the Plan wording must be used — and chapter 2 reports against that Plan, so the two cannot diverge.

## 3. The single largest analytical opportunity: transport AFC data

The brief asks for transport analysis to go beyond licensing statistics. The draft currently reports licences, fleet and the Gross Contract Model corridor results — but no passenger-demand analytics at all.

RURA already holds the necessary assets: a primary-routes GTFS dataset, an established AFC/e-ticketing relationship, and Gross Contract Model monitoring that is already producing ridership and revenue figures. The constraint is data access and processing time, not data existence.

With tap-in records (timestamp, route, stop, operator, vehicle, fare product, amount) for FY2024/25 and FY2025/26, the report could show: route-level demand against scheduled capacity, an hour-by-weekday demand profile, operator benchmarking on identical measures, fleet utilisation, load factors, and fare/ticketing compliance. Four of those are Priority 1 and achievable within the reporting timeline if the data feed is secured in the next two weeks.

This is the difference between a transport chapter that counts licences and one that explains how the network actually performs.

## 4. Regulatory effectiveness: activity vs outcome

The brief's distinction between activity and outcome is the report's central weakness — and the baseline already contains the fix.

The **Gross Contract Model case study** (chapter 4.6) is written in exactly the right shape: problem → action → result → remaining gap → next step. It is the best piece of regulatory writing in the draft. The **water expansion inspections** (3.4) have the same structure implicitly: 22 projects inspected, and the outcome attached (524,202 people served, 192 schools, 62 health facilities connected).

Everywhere else, the chain stops at output:

| Sector | Reported | Missing to complete the chain |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| ICT | Enforcement tools listed | What was found, compliance rate, directives, sanctions, whether QoS improved |
| Energy | Nothing | The entire chain |
| Water | 22 projects inspected (with outcomes) | Water quality compliance; operator inspections |
| Transport | 6 exercises, 1,689 offences | Enforcement measures by type; whether offences per 1,000 vehicles fell; safety outcome |
| Nuclear | 126 inspections | Findings by rating, corrective actions closed, dose exceedances |

**Recommendation:** adopt the case-study format as the standard for every significant intervention, and require two things of every compliance section: a compliance rate on one agreed cross-sector definition, and a directives-issued-vs-implemented count. Without an agreed definition, the Nuclear 23% and any future ICT or Energy figure will not be comparable — so the definition must be settled before the data is collected, not after.

## 5. What is genuinely blocked, and by how little

Three internal files unblock a disproportionate share of the empty cells:

| File | Unblocks |
| :-- | :-- |
| **M&E Framework (Excel)** | Chapter 2 in full (9 goals, 17 initiatives), annex 9.4, headline tile 15, and the target markers on every sector KPI card |
| **Annual Action Plan FY2025/26 end-year monitoring report** | Section 2.3 |
| **Finance pack + audit opinion** | Section 6.4, annex 9.3, headline tile 16, and the strategic goal 9 rating |

Four sections need nothing new at all — only a decision or an extraction from material RURA already holds:

- **2.4 Commitments from last year's report** — needs the published FY2024/25 report and a departmental response per commitment.
- **4.1 / 9.5 Register of regulatory instruments** — the source material is already scattered through chapters 3 and 6; it needs consolidating, not collecting.
- **7.1 Risk ratings** — the evidence column is complete; the nine risks need rating and assigning.
- **9.6 Indicator definitions** — needs writing down, and doing so structurally prevents a repeat of several of the 22 `CHECK` items next year.

These are the cheapest wins available and should move first.
